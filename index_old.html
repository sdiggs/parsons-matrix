Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, hover any point for its cell. Use the buttons to show or hide organizations; isolating one at a time is the clearest way to read its shape.

What the map shows so far

  • Six of eleven organizations work on repositories, and one works on integrators.Only the Data Foundation's CCED reaches Integrators, the organizations that combine datasets into products, and only on two floors. Funders are mostly paying for where data is kept, and almost no one on this map works on the products that combine it.
  • EDGI, PEDP, and OEDP fill the public-facing corner.With the Data Rescue Project, they make up a cluster on the Social science and EES floors that serves CBOs, the only four organizations here that do. EDGI formed in November 2016, PEDP in November 2024, and the Data Rescue Project in February 2025, each in response to federal data removals. Read against the rest of the cube, the institutions with stable funding sit away from communities, and the volunteer and coalition groups sit next to them.
  • PEDP rebuilds tools; the Data Rescue Project keeps copies.PEDP republished the Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool within days of its removal and now hosts EJScreen, EJAM, and HIFLD Next. That puts it at Interfaces as well as Repository, while the Data Rescue Project's main output is rescued datasets with a catalog on top.
  • OEDP is the only organization whose source is communities.Every other Source point on the map is an agency or research observing system. OEDP works with communities that collect their own environmental data and writes governance practices for it, which places it at Source and Standards.
  • KCF's PVSI holds both ends of the data chain.It works at Source (the Keeling Curve and other long-term observations) and at Interfaces (public tools such as the pull-the-plug simulator), and skips the middle. NYCE's DSI works at the access end of the same problem, so the two read as peers.
  • The Medicine floor now has three occupants.GREI holds funded infrastructure there; the Data Rescue Project and PEDP reach it through rescued CDC data, including the Social Vulnerability Index and Environmental Justice Index.

Open questions for reviewers

  • Who belongs on the Coordination row?CODAN, EDGI, and OEDP are there now. CRRDI, the AGU Data Resilience Project, and much of CCED's work also convene and coordinate. Moving them would empty much of the Repository row and change the first finding above.
  • Should coordinators be points or edges?An alternative is to drop the Coordination row and draw coordinators as links between the organizations they connect. The question gets sharper with PEDP on the map: EDGI and OEDP co-lead it with the Environmental Policy Innovation Center, so two points in the cube are also partners inside a third. The cube can't show links; a companion network view can.
  • How should home and reach be drawn?Generalist repositories accept any discipline's data, and CRRDI's mandate is cross-disciplinary while its funding comes from the health sector. Faint points are a first attempt.
  • Does GREI continue past 2026?Its listed project period runs 2022 to 2026, and an NIH Council of Councils working group has assessed its future.
