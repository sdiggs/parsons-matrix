Policy makers and Decision makers merged; no organization here serves one without the other. Funders moved off this axis, since they pay for the work rather than use the data, and now color the cube instead.

Home and reach

Home is what an organization exists to serve: the domains, functions, and users named in its charter, its mission, or its funder's terms. Reach is what its infrastructure touches without being designed for it.

The test: if the organization stopped serving it tomorrow, would its funder or board say it was failing at its job? If yes, it's home. If no, it's reach. NIH would count losing biomedical data as failure for GREI, so Health and Life are home; Dryad and Zenodo also hold climate data, but losing it wouldn't count, so Earth system is reach.

The test applies to all three axes. For now the cube draws reach only on the Domain axis.

What the map shows so far

  • Six of eleven organizations preserve data, and one integrates it.Only the Data Foundation's CCED works at Integrate, where datasets are combined into products. Funders on this map mostly pay for where data is kept, and almost no one works on the products that combine it.
  • Only one organization produces data.KCF's PVSI, through the Keeling Curve and long-term observations, is the only point on the Produce row. The observing systems themselves are federal programs that aren't on this map, which means every other organization here depends on producers it doesn't fund or govern.
  • The organizations closest to communities have the least secure funding.Color the cube by funder type. EDGI, PEDP, OEDP, and the Data Rescue Project are the only four that serve CBOs, and they run on volunteers, small grants, and philanthropy. The one federally funded organization, NIH's GREI, serves researchers in Health and Life, and its listed award period ends in 2026. CODAN coordinates with no funding at all.
  • Advocacy and training are now visible.EDGI, PEDP, and KCF's PVSI advocate; GREI, OEDP, and the Data Rescue Project train. On the old axes, this work was forced into rows that didn't describe it.
  • KCF's PVSI and NYCE's DSI are peers at opposite ends.PVSI produces and delivers; the DSI preserves and coordinates. They share the Earth system floor and the Researchers column and nothing else.
  • The Health floor has three occupants.GREI holds funded infrastructure there. The Data Rescue Project and PEDP reach it through rescued CDC data, including the Social Vulnerability Index and the Environmental Justice Index.

Open questions for reviewers

  • Do the revised axes hold up?The axis changes are proposals to the original figure's framework. They need sign-off from the people who drew it before this page treats them as settled.
  • How much funding does each organization have?Dot size is reserved for funding tier (under $250K, $250K to $2M, over $2M). It stays constant until there's a figure for every organization, since a size encoding with gaps would mislead.
  • Where do the funders sit?A next layer would plot funders' own portfolios in the same space: the domains, functions, and users each one pays for. Cells with organizations but no funder show work without support; cells with neither are the true gaps.
  • Should partnerships be drawn as links?EDGI and OEDP co-lead PEDP with the Environmental Policy Innovation Center, so two points in the cube are also partners inside a third. The cube can't show links; a companion network view can.
  • Does GREI continue past 2026?Its listed project period runs 2022 to 2026, and an NIH Council of Councils working group has assessed its future.
